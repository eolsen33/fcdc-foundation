Treatment costs a fraction of a prison cell — and it gives our neighbors their lives back.

Every year, Flagler County residents living with a substance use disorder come through our courts. Drug court offers them supervised treatment instead of incarceration. We are the local foundation that pays for the things the court budget doesn't cover — and the ones that decide whether someone finishes.

Where your tax dollars go

One year in a Florida prison cell costs about four and a half times what one year of drug court costs.

This is the part that persuades people who have never met anyone in recovery. Drug court is not the soft option — it is the cheap one.

Incarceration — Florida

$34,898

per person, per year

Florida Department of Corrections full operating cost — $95.61 per person per day. Actual figure for fiscal year 2024–25.

Drug court — Florida

$7,840

per participant, per year

Statewide average annual cost per drug court participant, from the Florida Legislature's own program evaluation office.

Where these numbers come from — and the honest caveat.

The incarceration figure is the Florida Department of Corrections' full operating cost per inmate for FY 2024–25, as adopted by the state's Criminal Justice Estimating Conference in February 2026 (Office of Economic and Demographic Research).

The drug court figure is the statewide average annual cost per participant reported by OPPAGA Report 14-02, published in 2014 — the most recent Florida-specific per-participant analysis we can point to. Those are 2014 dollars. Adjusted for inflation it would land somewhere near $10,000–$11,000 today, which still leaves drug court costing well under a third of a prison bed. We would rather show you the caveat than quietly compare two different years.

Club 100 — our monthly circle

$10 a month sustains one person's support for a year.

A single gift helps once. A monthly gift is what lets us tell a case manager yes in the middle of February, when someone needs a ride, a test, or a kit and there is no grant cycle coming.

Club 100 is exactly what it sounds like: a hundred neighbors giving $120 a year — $10 a month — so that the Foundation always has something in hand. It is the most useful money we receive.

Need help now?

  • 988 — Suicide & Crisis Lifeline. Call or text, 24/7.
  • 1-800-662-4357 — SAMHSA National Helpline. Free, confidential treatment referrals, 24/7.
  • 211 — Florida 211. Local treatment, housing, and food assistance.
  • If someone is overdosing right now, call 911. Florida's Good Samaritan law protects people who call for help.
