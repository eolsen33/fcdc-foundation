Volunteer with us

Our team is entirely local volunteers. We need help staffing events, handing out Narcan, and running the annual Ride for Recovery — and we need people willing to speak plainly about recovery in their own circles.

We are also short of specific skills: a bookkeeper, a grant writer, someone who enjoys fundraising, and an administrator. See the list below for what each one involves.

Use the form and choose Volunteering, and tell us what you are good at.

Use your own gifts

The specific help we are short of.

We want people with a heart for helping people — but you do not have to be the one in the room with a participant to do that. Most of what we are missing is back-office work, and most of it is a few hours a month.

A bookkeeper

We run about a dozen transactions in a busy month. A professional is helping us set the books up properly; after that it is a couple of hours, not a job.

A grant writer

Drafting the application is the easy part now. What we need is somebody who can find the grants that genuinely fit who we are, and read the conditions closely enough to know we can spend the money where it is actually needed.

Someone who enjoys fundraising

Two of the people who used to carry this had to step away. Sponsorships and events now sit on one desk, and that is the single biggest brake on what we can do for participants.

An administrator

Keeping the calendar, the equipment and the paperwork straight, so any volunteer can pick up a key and go set up an event without one particular person having to be there.

Event hands

Setting up and breaking down the Ride for Recovery, the golf tournament, First Friday in Flagler Beach, and the Narcan table at community events.

Board members

Our board is neighbors, not names. If you can bring a skill and a few hours a month, there is a seat at the table.

Or just support the work.

Giving takes a minute and funds a ride, a car part, or a kit this month.

Need help now?

  • 988 — Suicide & Crisis Lifeline. Call or text, 24/7.
  • 1-800-662-4357 — SAMHSA National Helpline. Free, confidential treatment referrals, 24/7.
  • 211 — Florida 211. Local treatment, housing, and food assistance.
  • If someone is overdosing right now, call 911. Florida's Good Samaritan law protects people who call for help.
