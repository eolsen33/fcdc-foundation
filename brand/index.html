02 — Typography

Lexend for headings, Source Sans 3 for body

Lexend was designed to improve reading proficiency; Source Sans 3 is a workhorse with excellent number shapes. Both are open source and self-hosted — no Google Fonts request.

Lexend 700 · headings · letter-spacing -0.015em · line-height 1.2

Recovery is possible here.

Source Sans 3 400 · body · 17px · line-height 1.65

The Flagler County Drug Court Foundation supports people in the drug court program with the practical things the court budget does not cover — a ride, a test, a kit, a graduation.

Scale

13 / 15 / 17 (body) / 19 / 22 / 28 / 34 / 44 px
Body is 17px, not 16. Deliberate — this audience skews older and reads on phones. Never go below 15px for anything but legal text.

Numbers

Dollar figures and statistics always use font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums so columns line up and figures don't jitter. The big cost figures are Lexend 700 at clamp(2.5rem, 9vw, 3.5rem).

03 — Logo

HOPE — Helping Our Participants Excel

Do

  • Give it clear space equal to the height of the "H" on every side.
  • On dark backgrounds, place it on a white rounded plate — the logo has no dark-mode variant.
  • Keep it at least 120px wide so "Helping Our Participants Excel" stays legible.
  • Pair it with the full legal name in text nearby for first-time visitors.

Don't

  • Recolour it, add effects, or separate the figure from the wordmark.
  • Place it directly on a photograph or a purple field.
  • Stretch it — the source is raster, so scale proportionally and never up past 720px.
  • Use it as a favicon substitute for the site's own icon at tiny sizes.

[ASSET GAP] The only logo available is a raster image recovered from the old website. Ask the Foundation whether an original vector (AI/EPS/SVG) exists. If not, a redraw is worth doing once — it would sharpen print, signage, event banners, and the app icon in one go.

04 — Voice & language

The non-negotiable part

Destigmatization is in the Foundation's mission statement. Copy that stigmatizes contradicts the client's own mission — this is a correctness rule, not a style preference.

Always say

  • "a person with a substance use disorder"
  • "substance use disorder"
  • "returned to use"
  • "a positive / negative test result"
  • "person in recovery", "participant", "graduate"
  • "medication for opioid use disorder"

Never say

  • "addict", "abuser", "junkie", "user"
  • "drug problem", "drug habit"
  • "relapsed"
  • "clean" / "dirty" — for people or test results
  • "offender" where "participant" will do
  • "substance abuser"

Tone

Plain, factual, unsentimental. This organization makes a fiscal argument as often as an emotional one — lead with the number when talking to taxpayers, and with the person when talking to families. Never beg. Never use urgency theatre.

Storytelling rules

No before/after framing. No mugshots. No rock-bottom narrative arcs. No redemption porn. Any participant or graduate story requires a written, signed release — substance use treatment records are protected under 42 CFR Part 2, which is stricter than HIPAA. Never write a fictional testimonial, even as a sample.

05 — Imagery

What we photograph, and what we refuse to

Use

  • Real Flagler County places and real volunteers
  • Hands, groups, tables at community events
  • Daylight, open air, ordinary settings
  • The Foundation's own event photography wherever possible

Never use

  • Stock photos of people in visible distress
  • Needles, pills, powder, paraphernalia
  • Handcuffs, cell bars, mugshot framing
  • Silhouetted "anonymous addict" clichés

Technical: ship WebP with a JPEG fallback, always set width and height, and lazy-load anything below the fold. The old site served a single 1.6 MB homepage graphic; the equivalent here is 20 KB.

Placeholder

[CLIENT TO SUPPLY] Hatched amber block. Impossible to miss in review, and impossible to mistake for finished copy.

Accessibility floor

  • Body text contrast 4.5:1 minimum; headings and figures clear 7:1
  • Visible 3px focus ring on every interactive element — never remove it
  • 44×44px minimum tap targets, 8px apart
  • Never convey meaning by colour alone — the cost bars carry text labels too
  • Respect prefers-reduced-motion
  • Every meaningful image gets real alt text; decorative ones get alt=""
